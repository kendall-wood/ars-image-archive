Abstract:

The research within this project examines the intersection of sociological and psychological impacts on the development, behavior, and well-being of members of the black community residing in lower-income and urban communities. Specifically, how does the impact of generational trauma, household and larger environment stress, nutritional access, and institutional barriers compound and influence daily activity and behavior? The direct biological effects of generational trauma and our emerging understanding of the “baggage” of DNA has an immediate effect on one’s behavior and well-being. Systemic barriers towards proper healthcare, nutritional access, exposure to good habits, and affordability are understood as necessities towards the wellbeing of any human being, yet media cycles have largely disregarded these causes in their commentary on the African-American community. The conversation of personal responsibility against systemic conditions is an ongoing debate amongst the American political climate in relation to the perspective of the urban Black community, but how do systemic conditions inform one’s capacity and perspective for personal responsibility? The historical and active demonization of the black community by the American system of white supremacy has always existed in disregard to the conditions African-Americans are placed in by those that demonize them. Notions of racial separation and hierarchy both biologically and socially render humanistic perspectives on the Black community nearly impossible. This paper overcomes these surface level and manipulated distinctions through a compiling and cross-analysis of psychological, sociological, economic, and public health research in attempts to implement a more human and contextual perspective towards the developmental and biological well-being of members of the African-American community. How can we examine individual actions within a broader context when we account truly for the physiological needs of a human and the implications of deficiencies of such? This paper examines how the active effects and historical ramifications of inequality shape a community and individuals while establishing a new framework for social observation.
